# The Purely Personal Hook Library -- Layer 2

Purely Personal · by Daniel Paul

Seven hook families, fifteen patterns, a little over a hundred templates for
the hook line on a short-form video. The patterns are the ones the Purely
Personal hook system already uses for LinkedIn -- detection, warning,
personal pivot, contrarian reframe, result lead, bold declaration, curiosity
gap, identity mirror, before/after, authority proof, question, confession --
plus three this plugin adds for video: the challenge, the open loop and the
direct promise. Every template here is original to Purely Personal.

This is **Layer 2**: the topping on a hook that already passes Layer 1 (see
`hook-standards.md`). It never replaces Layer 1. If no template fits what the
speaker actually says, skip Layer 2; never bend the video to fit a template.

## How to use it

1. Read the transcript and decide what the video **does**: teach a method,
   bust a belief, name a struggle, show a result, tell a story, reveal
   something. That picks the family -- see the table below.
2. Inside the family, pick the pattern whose **trigger** matches the strongest
   thing in the video. The trigger is why a hook works; the wording is only
   one way to pull it.
3. Fill the brackets with this video's specifics: its words, its numbers, its
   viewer. A real detail ("3 posts", "40 cold DMs", "in German") beats any
   clever phrasing.
4. Run the result back through Layer 1.

| The video... | Reach for |
|---|---|
| teaches a method or a how-to | 7 Promise, 5 Proof |
| argues against common advice | 2 Contrarian |
| names a struggle the viewer has | 3 Mirror, 4 Warning |
| shows a result or a transformation | 5 Proof |
| is the speaker's own story | 6 Story |
| has a surprising finding or twist | 1 Curiosity, 6 Open loop |

The three options shown to the user come from **three different families**,
picked from this table for this video. Do not fall back on the same three
every time; a video with a story or a result in it is wasting it on a generic
promise.

## Rules for every hook

From the Purely Personal voice:

- **Specific beats general.** "3 clients", not "several clients". "Founders who
  post every day", not "people".
- **Short words, short sentences.** It is read in about two seconds while the
  speaker is already talking.
- **Written for one viewer.** "You", never "you all" or "many people".
- **No AI tells.** Never: leverage, unlock, elevate, empower, navigate, delve,
  game-changer, innovative, cutting-edge, transformative, seamlessly. No em
  dashes. No exclamation marks for enthusiasm.
- **No question as a lazy opener.** A question hook earns its place only when
  the viewer answers it silently and does not like their answer.

**Another language:** the templates are English. For a German (or any other)
video, keep the pattern -- the trigger and the structure -- and write the line
the way a native speaker would say it. Never translate a template word for word.

## The bracket key

| Bracket | Fill with |
|---|---|
| [WHO] | the exact viewer: "solo founders", "coaches on LinkedIn" |
| [RESULT] | what they want: clients, views, a finished video, time back |
| [PROBLEM] | the pain or the obstacle |
| [MISTAKE] | the error they are probably making right now |
| [HABIT] | the routine that is holding them back |
| [METHOD] | the speaker's approach, tool or system |
| [COMMON ADVICE] | what everyone in the niche says |
| [THING] | the object of the video: a post, a profile, a hook, a video |
| [TOOL] | the app or platform involved |
| [NUMBER], [TIME] | a real figure or timeframe from the video |
| [BEFORE], [AFTER] | the two states the video moves between |
| [COST] | what the mistake is quietly costing them |

## 1. Curiosity -- the viewer needs to close a gap

### 1.1 The curiosity gap
**Trigger:** something surprising is set up and the answer is held back.
**Test:** can the viewer guess what comes next? If yes, rewrite.

1. The [THING] that got me [RESULT] took [SHORT TIME] to make.
2. Nobody in [NICHE] does this one thing. That's why it works.
3. My best [THING] broke every rule about [TOPIC].
4. [NUMBER] [WHO] asked me the same question this week. Here's the answer.
5. There's a reason your [THING] [BAD RESULT], and it isn't [OBVIOUS GUESS].
6. What [ADMIRED TYPE OF PERSON] does before every [ACTION]. Almost nobody copies it.
7. One setting in [TOOL] changed how my [THING] [PERFORMS].

### 1.2 The question
**Trigger:** the viewer answers in their head and feels the gap.
**Test:** is the honest answer slightly uncomfortable?

1. When did [GOOD THING] last happen without you chasing it?
2. How many of your [THINGS] actually led to [RESULT]?
3. Would your [THING] still work without [CRUTCH]?
4. What does your [THING] say about you in the first [NUMBER] seconds?
5. If [CHANNEL] disappeared tomorrow, what would you have left?
6. Why does [THING] feel harder now than when you started?
7. Is your [THING] built for the [WHO] you want, or the ones you have?

## 2. Contrarian -- it goes against what they were told

### 2.1 The contrarian reframe
**Trigger:** cognitive dissonance; the advice they follow is challenged.
**Test:** does the right viewer feel a little defensive? Good.

1. Forget [COMMON ADVICE]. [METHOD] is what gets you [RESULT].
2. [COMMON ADVICE] sounds smart. It's costing you [RESULT].
3. Stop [POPULAR ACTION]. Start [OPPOSITE ACTION].
4. Everyone says [COMMON ADVICE]. Here's why I don't.
5. [POPULAR ACTION] isn't getting you [RESULT]. This is.
6. The worst advice in [NICHE]: "[COMMON ADVICE]."
7. Doing less [ACTION] got me more [RESULT].

### 2.2 The bold declaration
**Trigger:** authority plus provocation, stated as fact with no hedging.
**Test:** would someone want to argue with it?

1. [ACCEPTED THING] is [BLUNT REFRAME].
2. [VANITY METRIC] means nothing if [REAL GOAL] isn't happening.
3. Most advice about [TOPIC] is [BLUNT LABEL].
4. [NICHE] doesn't have a [SURFACE] problem. It has a [REAL] problem.
5. Your [THING] isn't [WHAT THEY THINK]. It's [WHAT IT REALLY IS].
6. [TOPIC] is simple. It gets made complicated so it can be sold.
7. Nobody cares about your [THING]. They care about [WHAT THEY GET].

## 3. Mirror -- "how did they know that about me?"

### 3.1 The detection
**Trigger:** pattern recognition; a specific thing the viewer does, and a
consequence they have not named yet.
**Test:** does the viewer think "that is exactly my situation"?

1. You [GOOD ACTION] every [TIME]. [RESULT] still isn't coming. Here's why.
2. You keep getting [WRONG RESULT] instead of [RIGHT RESULT]. That's a [REAL CAUSE] problem.
3. You've rewritten your [THING] [NUMBER] times. The words were never the issue.
4. Your [THING] gets [VANITY RESULT] and zero [REAL RESULT].
5. You open [TOOL], stare at it, and close it again. This fixes that.
6. You sound sharp on a call. Then you [ACTION] and go blank.
7. You started [THING] to [GOAL]. Now it eats [TIME] a week.

### 3.2 The identity mirror
**Trigger:** recognition and relief; "finally, someone gets it".
**Test:** does the viewer feel seen rather than sold to?

1. You're good at what you do. Your [CHANNEL] just doesn't show it.
2. You're doing everything right and nothing is working. That's not a [EFFORT] problem.
3. If [TASK] feels like homework, this is for you.
4. For the [WHO] who hates [ACTION] but knows they need it.
5. Being bad at [THING] isn't your problem. [REAL CAUSE] is.
6. [WHO]: this is the [THING] I wish I'd had in year one.
7. You already have [ASSET]. Nobody knows about it yet.

## 4. Warning -- they might be losing something right now

### 4.1 The warning
**Trigger:** loss aversion; a mistake that is costing them as they watch.
**Test:** does the viewer immediately want to check whether they do this?

1. If your [THING] opens with [COMMON OPENING], you lose [WHO] in the first second.
2. [ACTION] before [PREREQUISITE] is like [VIVID ANALOGY].
3. Every time you [HABIT], [COST].
4. Don't [ACTION] until you've [PREREQUISITE].
5. The [THING] mistake that quietly costs [WHO] [COST].
6. Delete this from your [THING] today.
7. If you use [TOOL] like this, [RESULT] will never come.

### 4.2 The challenge
**Trigger:** a dare; small effort, visible payoff, and pride in doing what
others will not.
**Test:** is the action small enough that the viewer could do it today?

1. Put this in your next [THING]. Then compare the [METRIC].
2. [SHORT TIME]. One [ACTION]. That's all this takes.
3. Do this before your next [THING]. Then tell me it didn't work.
4. Most [WHO] won't do this. That's the whole advantage.
5. Copy this [THING] exactly. Change one word: [WORD].
6. Watch this, then go and look at your own [THING].
7. It takes [SHORT TIME] and almost everyone skips it.

## 5. Proof -- a result they cannot ignore

### 5.1 The result lead
**Trigger:** proof plus curiosity; a number that demands the explanation.
**Test:** is the number surprising for the effort it took?

1. [NUMBER] [THINGS]. [BIG RESULT]. One change.
2. [SMALL INPUT] did more for my [RESULT] than [BIG INPUT] ever did.
3. [RESULT] in [TIME], without [EXPECTED REQUIREMENT].
4. This [THING] got [NUMBER] [RESULT]. Here's exactly why.
5. One [SMALL CHANGE]. [NUMBER]% more [RESULT].
6. [WHO] went from [NUMBER] to [NUMBER] [RESULT]. Same [CONSTANT].
7. The [THING] behind [RESULT]. It took [SHORT TIME].

### 5.2 Before and after
**Trigger:** juxtaposition; the gap between two states asks to be explained.
**Test:** is the gap big enough that "how?" is the only reaction?

1. [MONTH]: [BEFORE]. [MONTH]: [AFTER].
2. Before: [BEFORE]. After: [AFTER]. Same [CONSTANT].
3. [BEFORE NUMBER] to [AFTER NUMBER] in [TIME]. One change in between.
4. My [THING] [TIME] ago, and my [THING] today.
5. Same [CONSTANT]. Different [VARIABLE]. [RESULT GAP].
6. Day 1: [BEFORE]. Day [NUMBER]: [AFTER].
7. Left: [BEFORE]. Right: [AFTER]. Here's what changed.

### 5.3 The authority proof
**Trigger:** social proof with a twist; experience earns the stop, the
reversal earns the watch.
**Test:** is the authority real -- a number from the video, not a claim?

1. I've [DONE THING] for [NUMBER] [WHO]. The same mistake shows up every time.
2. After [NUMBER] [THINGS], this is the one rule I still follow.
3. [NUMBER] years of [THING] taught me one thing about [TOPIC].
4. I've seen [NUMBER] [THINGS] this year. The best ones share one thing.
5. My client got [RESULT]. They didn't change [EXPECTED THING].
6. What [NUMBER] [THINGS] taught me about [TOPIC].
7. The first question every [WHO] I work with asks.

## 6. Story -- the speaker has been where the viewer is

### 6.1 The personal pivot
**Trigger:** curiosity about what changed, plus proof that it can.
**Test:** does the viewer want to know the one thing?

1. [TIME] ago I was [WRONG ACTION]. [BAD RESULT]. Then I changed one thing.
2. The day I stopped [HABIT], [RESULT] started.
3. I nearly gave up on [THING]. This is what changed my mind.
4. [TIME] ago I couldn't [ACTION]. Now [WHO] pay me to do it.
5. What finally made [THING] click, after [TIME] of getting it wrong.
6. I did [THING] the hard way for [TIME]. Here's the easy way.
7. One conversation changed how I [ACTION].

### 6.2 The confession
**Trigger:** vulnerability; "I've done that too".
**Test:** is it something the viewer has done but would not admit?

1. I spent [TIME] on [THING]. Not one [RESULT] came from it.
2. I used to [WRONG HABIT]. Here's what it cost me.
3. The thing about [TOPIC] I'm most embarrassed I believed.
4. I got [TOPIC] wrong for [TIME]. Don't copy me.
5. Honest truth: I [UNEXPECTED ADMISSION].
6. I used to spend [LONG TIME] on [TASK]. Now it takes [SHORT TIME].
7. My worst [THING] taught me more than my best one.

### 6.3 The open loop
**Trigger:** an unfinished thought; the viewer stays to see it closed.
**Test:** is the payoff real and inside this video? An open loop the video
never closes breaks trust, and Layer 1.

1. This sounds wrong. Wait for the second half.
2. Part one is the problem. Part two is the bit you'll save.
3. I [UNUSUAL ACTION] for [TIME]. Nobody warned me about the result.
4. I tested [THING] so you don't have to. The result surprised me.
5. The answer to [COMMON QUESTION] isn't [EXPECTED ANSWER].
6. This is the [THING] I almost didn't share.
7. [NUMBER] seconds. That's how long [THING] takes once you see this.
8. Everyone gets the first step right. Step two is where it breaks.

## 7. Promise -- exactly what they get

### 7.1 The direct promise
**Trigger:** clarity; the viewer knows at once it is for them and what they
will walk away with. The most Layer-1-safe family: when in doubt, start here.
**Test:** could the viewer say, in their own words, what this video gives them?

1. This is how you [RESULT] without [PAIN].
2. How to [RESULT] in [SHORT TIME], even if [OBJECTION].
3. The [NUMBER]-step [METHOD] for [RESULT].
4. Use this next time you [SITUATION].
5. [WHO]: here's how to [RESULT] this week.
6. Steal my [THING] for [RESULT].
7. Exactly what to say when [SITUATION].
8. The fastest way to [RESULT] if you [CONSTRAINT].
