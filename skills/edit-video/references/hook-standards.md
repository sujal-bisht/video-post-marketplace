# Writing the hook (the context line)

The hook is the line of text on screen for the first 4.5 seconds of a
short-form video. The speaker says their own opening; the hook tells the
viewer, in writing, **this video is for you, and here is what is in it for you.**

It is written from the transcript, never from a guess. Read the whole thing
first and answer two questions before writing a word:

- **What is this video about?**
- **What value is the speaker giving the viewer?** The takeaway, the fix, the
  shift in thinking -- the reason someone would be glad they watched.

Every hook is built in two layers, in this order.

## Layer 1 -- the foundation. Never skipped.

The hook must be **dumb-proof**: a viewer scrolling past at speed understands
it at once. It meets all three of these:

1. **It makes the viewer realise the video is for them.** The right person
   sees it and thinks "that's me".
2. **It tells them what is actually in it for them.** Enough context that they
   know what they are about to get.
3. **It makes them want to keep watching to the end.** Enough pull that this
   is something they want, or need, to hear.

Measured against real examples:

- *"You don't need a personal brand."* **Passes.** Every founder has been told,
  everywhere, to build a personal brand; a line saying the opposite is
  instantly for them, and they have to know why.
- *"This is how you write unskippable personal story posts."* **Passes.** It
  names who it is for (anyone writing personal story posts) and what they get
  (how to make them unskippable).
- *"Brilliant in conversation, blank in content."* **Fails.** Clever, but the
  viewer cannot tell whether it is for them or what they will get.

**A hook that fails Layer 1 does not work at all, however clever it sounds.**

## Layer 2 -- the topping. Optional.

Only once a hook passes Layer 1, sharpen it with a pattern from
`hook-library.md`: more curiosity, a bolder claim, a cliffhanger, a mirror held
up to the viewer. Pick the family that fits what the speaker is actually
saying; fill the brackets from the transcript.

A hook without Layer 2 can still work well. Skip it when no pattern fits
rather than forcing one.

## The re-check -- not optional

After Layer 2, run the result back through Layer 1, all three points. Making a
hook catchier very easily makes it vaguer -- the clever version that lost who
it is for, or what is in it. If Layer 2 broke Layer 1, go back to the Layer 1
version. Foundation beats topping, every time.

## Length: emphasis first

There is no fixed word limit. The test is the emphasis -- the part of the line
that lands the point:

- If words can be cut and **the same emphasis stays**, cut them.
- If cutting would lose the emphasis, **leave it as it is.**

On screen, about 4-12 words fits in two or three lines; the layout breaks the
lines for itself and shrinks a little if it must. Write the line that works,
not the line that fits a count.

## Language

**The hook is written in the same language as the video.** German video,
German hook. Never translate, and never default to English.

## Spoken words are not the hook

The speaker's own first sentence is usually not the hook. It is often
throat-clearing ("Day 2.", "So today..."), and the hook sits on screen while
they say it -- repeating it word for word adds nothing. Write the line that
frames the whole video.

## What you show the user

Three options, each passing Layer 1, each from a **different** Layer 2 family,
so they are genuinely different choices. Choose the families from what THIS
video does (the table in `hook-library.md`), not out of habit: in testing, the
same three families -- promise, contrarian, mirror -- came out for every video
until this rule was written down. A story video wants a story hook; a video
with a result in it wants a proof hook. One line each, nothing else:

> 1. You don't need a personal brand.
> 2. Everyone says build a personal brand. Here's why you shouldn't.
> 3. Stop building a personal brand. Do this instead.

The user picks one, edits one, or writes their own. Whatever they choose goes
in as-is -- it is their video.
